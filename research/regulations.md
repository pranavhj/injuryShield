## Key Implications for InjuryShield

### Phase 1: Training/Practice Market (UNIVERSAL)
- Every league allows wearables in training
- This is our initial market — zero regulatory barriers
- Coaches have full access to data during practice
- No league approval needed

### Phase 2: In-Competition (Requires League Approval)
- **Easiest leagues:** NFL, MLS, FIFA, AFL, Premier League, PGA, Cricket — already allow wearables
- **Hard leagues:** NBA (banned), NHL (banned), World Rugby (HR banned)
- **MLB:** Only 3 specific devices approved. 6-8 month approval timeline.
- **NCAA:** New guidelines coming 2026 — opportunity to be early

### Regulatory Strategy
1. Start with training market (no barriers)
2. Apply for FIFA EPTS certification (opens global soccer market)
3. Get on NBA approved wearables list (training access to 30 NBA teams)
4. Apply for MLB device approval (6-8 month process)
5. Monitor NBA/NHL for rule changes allowing in-game wearables (trend is toward allowing)

### Data Privacy Issues
- NCAA emphasizing data governance for youth athletes (minors)
- Athletes' data ownership is legally unclear globally
- NBA: data "may not be used in contract negotiations" ($250K fine)
- GDPR applies in Europe (Premier League, Bundesliga)
- We MUST have clear data ownership policies from day 1

**Goal: Get InjuryShield on this list.**

### IEEE P3716 Standard
IEEE is developing standard P3716 for "objective performance evaluation of electronic systems that measure speed, position and velocity in field- and court-based sports." Being compliant with this from day 1 would be a competitive advantage.

### Size/Weight Restrictions
- No league specifies exact weight limits (found)
- General requirement: "must not pose safety risk to athlete or opponents"
- Sensors must not have sharp edges or hard protrusions
- Must not provide competitive advantage through illegal means (e.g., communication)
- FIFA EPTS: specific certification process for lower-limb wearables
